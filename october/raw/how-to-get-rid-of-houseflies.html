How to Get Rid of Houseflies: 12 Natural Ways That Work

The fastest answer to how to get rid of houseflies is to remove what they breed and feed on: open bins, uncovered food, wet waste and dirty drains. Sprays and swatters deal with the flies you can see, but how to get rid of houseflies for good depends on hygiene, because new flies keep hatching from any rotting matter nearby. This guide covers how to get rid of houseflies in 12 natural ways, starting with the hygiene fixes, then simple deterrents such as neem, then the signs that you need a professional. If you have tried every step on how to get rid of houseflies and they still fill the kitchen, the source is probably somewhere you have not looked yet.

Why do houseflies keep coming into your home?

Houseflies follow smell. Rotting food, a full bin, pet waste or a blocked drain draws them in through any open door or window, and the same material is where they lay eggs. In warm weather a housefly can go from egg to adult in around a week, so a bin left for a few days can produce its own flies inside your home.

They matter for more than annoyance. Flies land on waste and then on food, plates and counters, and can carry germs from one to the other. That is why every method below starts in the kitchen.

Is it really a housefly, or a different small fly?

Not every fly in the kitchen is a housefly, and each type breeds somewhere different. Matching the fly to its source saves you treating the wrong spot.

FlyHow to recognise itWhere it breeds
HouseflyGrey, about 6 to 7 mm long, four dark stripes on the backGarbage, rotting food, wet waste, animal dung
Fruit flyTiny and tan, often with red eyes, hovers over fruitOverripe fruit, juice spills, empty bottles
Drain flyTiny, fuzzy, moth-like wings, rests on bathroom wallsThe slimy film inside drains and floor traps
Fungus gnatTiny and dark, flies around potted plantsDamp potting soil

If you mostly see the smaller three, the fix is a fruit bowl moved to the fridge, a scrubbed drain or drier plant soil. The rest of this guide is aimed at houseflies.

Kitchen and bathroom pest control tips: ways 1 to 6

These six steps take away the breeding and feeding sites. Do them for two weeks before you judge anything else, since flies already hatching will keep appearing for a few days.

1. Lid the bin and empty it daily

Use a bin with a tight lid, line it, and take the wet waste out every evening. Wash the bin with soap and hot water once a week and let it dry in the sun, because the residue at the bottom is enough to attract flies even when the bag is new.

2. Cover food and clear the table

Keep cooked food covered, put leftovers in the fridge within a couple of hours, and do not leave plates or tea cups on the table. Move ripe fruit into the fridge or a covered basket.

3. Clean the kitchen surfaces flies feed on

Wipe the counter, stove and sink edge after each meal, and empty the sink strainer every night. Clean grease from the chimney filter and the wall behind the stove, as greasy films hold food smells. Our kitchen cleaning tips set out a daily and weekly routine.

4. Keep bathrooms and drains dry and clean

For bathroom pest control, clear hair from floor drains, scrub the drain grate with a brush once a week, and fix leaking taps and flush valves. Squeegee the floor after bathing so water does not stand overnight. Our bathroom deep cleaning checklist covers the spots that get missed.

5. Clean up pet waste and bird droppings quickly

Pick up pet waste on the balcony or terrace the same day, rinse pet food bowls after meals, and wash bird droppings off ledges. Flies lay eggs in dung within hours in warm weather.

6. Manage compost and wet waste

If you compost at home, keep the bin closed, cover each layer of kitchen waste with dry leaves or cocopeat, and keep the mix moist but not soggy. A wet, smelly compost bin becomes a fly nursery.

Natural ways to keep pests away: ways 7 to 12

Once the breeding sites are gone, these steps keep the remaining flies out or away from food. None of them works on its own if the bin is still open.

7. Fit mesh on windows and doors

Insect mesh on kitchen windows and balcony doors keeps most flies out while letting air in. Check the edges for gaps, and keep the main door shut rather than propped open during cooking.

8. Use neem for pest control at home

Mix about a teaspoon of neem oil and a few drops of liquid soap into a litre of water, shake well and spray around the bin area, window sills and balcony corners every few days. Neem makes those surfaces less attractive to flies; it does not kill a breeding population. Keep the spray off food and plates, and test it on a hidden patch of painted or wooden surfaces first.

9. Grow tulsi, mint or lemongrass near openings

Pots of tulsi, mint or lemongrass on the kitchen window or near the balcony door give off scents flies tend to avoid. The effect is mild, so treat the plants as a small extra, not a fix.

10. Cloves in a cut lemon

Push a few cloves into half a lemon and keep it on the dining table or near the fruit basket. It can keep flies off that one spot for a day or two, after which you replace it.

11. Keep the air moving

Flies find it hard to land in a steady breeze. A ceiling or pedestal fan over the dining table or the cooking area keeps them off food while you eat or cook.

12. Catch the rest with simple traps

Sticky fly ribbons hung away from food, or a bottle trap made by cutting a plastic bottle and turning the top upside down into the base with some sugar water inside, catch the flies already indoors. A swatter handles strays. Vinegar jar traps catch fruit flies far better than houseflies.

Safety note: neem oil, essential oils and strong-smelling remedies should stay out of reach of babies, children and pets, and never go on food. If you use any fly spray, follow the label on dose and ventilation, and keep it away from open food and cooking surfaces. How do you prevent pests in an apartment building?

In a flat, some flies come from sources you do not control: the garbage collection point, a neighbour's balcony, or a shared shaft. You can still reduce them with a few steps.

  • Ask your society to keep the bin area covered, washed and cleared on a fixed schedule, and to fix leaking garbage chutes.
  • Do not leave your bin bag outside the door overnight if collection is in the morning.
  • Check the duct or shaft behind kitchen and bathroom windows for dumped waste or bird nests.
  • Keep the gap under the main door small with a door seal, which also helps against cockroaches.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are there so many flies in my house after the rains?

Rain soaks garbage and organic waste, and the warm, humid weather after it speeds up fly breeding. Wet waste rots faster, and blocked drains and puddles near the building add more breeding sites. Emptying bins daily and keeping the kitchen dry through this period cuts the numbers quickly.

Can houseflies make food unsafe?

Yes. Flies feed and lay eggs on garbage and dung, then land on food and plates, so they can carry germs onto anything left uncovered. Keep cooked food covered, refrigerate leftovers promptly and wipe counters before you start cooking. Throw away food that flies have been sitting on for a long time.

Do fly sprays work better than natural methods?

A spray kills the flies it reaches, so it gives quick relief, but it does nothing about the bin or drain producing new ones. Natural methods and hygiene take a little longer and stop the cycle. If you use a spray, follow the label, keep it away from food and children, and ventilate the room afterwards.

Is neem oil safe around children and pets?

Diluted neem oil sprayed on window sills and bin areas is a low-risk option, but it should not be swallowed or sprayed where children play or pets eat. Store the bottle out of reach and keep the spray off toys, food bowls and plates. If a child or pet swallows neem oil, call a doctor or vet.

Why do flies gather on my balcony but not inside?

Balconies often hold the things flies want: a bin waiting for collection, pet waste, bird droppings, plant pots with rotting leaves or a drain that holds water. Flies also come from neighbouring balconies and the building's garbage area. Clear and wash your balcony, then check what sits just below or next to it.
