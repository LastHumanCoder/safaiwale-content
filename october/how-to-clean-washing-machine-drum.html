How to Clean Washing Machine Drum, Fridge and AC at Home

Here is how to clean washing machine drum at home in one line: unplug it, clear the rubber seal and filters, then run an empty hot cycle with the cleaner your manual recommends. Front load and top load machines differ in the details, so below we show how to clean washing machine drum for each, step by step. If you looked up how to clean washing machine drum because of a musty smell or grey flecks on clean clothes, the seal and drain filter are usually the cause. Once you know how to clean washing machine drum safely, the same unplug-first routine works for the fridge, microwave, AC filter and ceiling fan, which this guide also covers.

Unplug first: safety rules for every appliance

Before you clean any appliance

  • Unplug it at the socket. For hard-wired units like split ACs and exhaust fans, switch off the MCB as well.
  • Keep water away from control panels, motors, wiring and plugs. Wring cloths out until barely damp.
  • Let it cool. Wait for a microwave, motor or drum to cool before touching the inside.
  • Never mix bleach with vinegar, lemon or other acid cleaners, or with ammonia. The fumes are toxic.
  • Follow the manufacturer manual. It names the cleaners your model can take and any that void the warranty. Most brands put manuals on their websites if you have lost the paper copy.

How to clean washing machine drum: front load

Front loaders smell when water and detergent sit in the door seal and drain filter. Work through these steps once a month if you wash daily.

  1. Clean the rubber door seal. Pull back the folds and wipe out hair, coins and grey slime with a cloth and warm soapy water. For black mould spots, use only a cleaner your manual allows on the seal.
  2. Soak the detergent drawer. Most drawers pull out with a press tab. Soak them in warm soapy water, scrub with an old toothbrush and wipe the cavity they sit in.
  3. Run the drum clean programme. Most machines have a Drum Clean or Tub Clean setting. Run it empty with the drum cleaner your manual recommends, or with nothing if it says so. Some manufacturers advise against vinegar because it can wear rubber parts, so check before using it.
  4. Clear the drain pump filter. With the machine unplugged and cool, open the small panel at the bottom front. Put a shallow tray and towels down first, because water will pour out. Twist the filter out, remove lint and debris, rinse and refit firmly.
  5. Leave the door and drawer open after every wash so the inside dries.

Top load and semi-automatic machines

Top loaders build up scale and detergent crust under the rim and around the agitator or pulsator.

  1. Run the Tub Clean programme if there is one, with the cleaner your manual names. If there is not, fill the tub with the warmest water the machine allows, add the cleaner, let it agitate for a minute, pause it for the soak time on the cleaner's pack, then finish the cycle.
  2. Pull out the lint filter, usually a mesh pouch on the drum wall or a cup on the pulsator, and wash it under the tap.
  3. Scrub under the rim and the lid hinge with a toothbrush and soapy water.

Semi-automatic machines have two tubs. Clean the wash tub as above, wipe the spin tub, clear the lint filter and run water through the drain hose to flush it. Homes on hard borewell water may see white scale return faster; a descaler approved in your manual handles that.

How to clean refrigerator deeply

  1. Unplug and empty it. Move perishables into a cool box or a neighbour's fridge, and throw out anything past its date.
  2. Defrost if needed. Direct-cool fridges build ice in the freezer. Let it melt with towels below; never chip it off with a knife, as you can puncture the cooling pipes.
  3. Wash shelves and drawers in warm soapy water. Let cold glass shelves reach room temperature first, because hot water on cold glass can crack it.
  4. Wipe the inside with a tablespoon of baking soda dissolved in a litre of warm water. It cleans without leaving a smell near food.
  5. Scrub the door gasket with a toothbrush and the same solution, then dry the folds where mould starts.
  6. Clear the drain hole. In frost-free fridges, a small hole at the back of the fridge section drains defrost water. Flush it gently with warm water from a syringe so water does not pool under the crisper.
  7. Dust the coils at the back or underneath with a brush and vacuum, keeping it unplugged.

Dry everything, plug it back in and wait until it is cold before reloading. The warm space behind and under a fridge also attracts cockroaches; if you see droppings there, our cockroach control service starts with a free inspection.

How to clean microwave naturally

Steam does the scrubbing for you. Half fill a wide microwave-safe bowl with water, add the juice of a lemon or two spoons of vinegar, and heat it until the window steams up. Leave the door shut for five minutes so the steam softens dried food, then open it carefully, lift the bowl out with an oven glove and wipe the walls and roof with a damp cloth. Wash the turntable and roller ring in soapy water. Never run the microwave empty, and never use steel wool inside, which scratches the coating and can spark.

How to clean air conditioner filter

  1. Switch the AC off with the remote, then at the switch or MCB.
  2. Lift the front panel of the indoor unit and slide out the mesh filters.
  3. Vacuum the dusty side, then rinse with lukewarm water from the clean side so dust is pushed out, not deeper in. Use a drop of mild soap if the filter is greasy.
  4. Dry the filters fully in the shade before refitting. Never run the AC without them.

Your manual gives the right interval; filters in a dusty city in peak summer clog much faster. Do not spray water into the indoor unit or onto the coil yourself. Coil, drain tray and gas checks need a technician, so book our AC cleaning service or the local page for AC cleaning in Delhi NCR.

How to clean ceiling fan quickly

Switch the fan off at the wall and wait until the blades stop completely. Stand on a stable ladder, not a chair. Slide an old pillowcase over each blade and pull it back towards you, so the dust falls inside the case instead of onto the floor. Then wipe each blade with a cloth dampened in mild soapy water, which matters for kitchen fans coated in grease, and dry them. Do not press down hard or bend the blades, because a bent blade makes the fan wobble. Wipe the motor housing with a dry cloth only.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I put bleach in my washing machine to kill mould?

Only if your manual allows it, and never at the same time as vinegar or an acid descaler. Some manufacturers approve diluted bleach for a drum clean, while others say it damages seals. When in doubt, use a drum cleaner made for washing machines and wipe the door seal by hand.

Why does my washing machine still smell after a drum clean?

The smell usually comes from places the cycle does not reach: the folds of the door seal, the drain pump filter or the detergent drawer cavity. Clean those by hand, then leave the door and drawer open between washes. Using too much detergent also leaves residue, so measure it to the load.

How often should I clean my fridge, microwave and AC filter?

Wipe spills in the fridge as they happen and deep clean it every two to three months. Steam-clean the microwave weekly if you use it daily. Check AC filters as often as your manual says, and more often in dusty, high-use summer months.

Can I wash my AC's indoor coil myself?

We do not recommend it. Water sprayed into the indoor unit can reach the electronics, and the coil fins bend easily. A technician cleans the coil and drain tray with the right equipment and checks the gas, which you cannot do at home. Stick to the filters yourself.

What is the safest natural cleaner for kitchen appliances?

Warm water with a few drops of dish liquid handles most grease, and a baking soda solution is ideal inside the fridge because it leaves no smell. Lemon water steam works for microwaves. Keep vinegar off stone counters and rubber seals unless your manual says it is safe, and never mix it with bleach.
