How to Clean After Diwali Crackers: Soot, Ash and Smell

Knowing how to clean after Diwali crackers comes down to order: collect the debris safely, lift soot and ash while they are dry, then wash, and deal with the smell last. If you are working out how to clean after Diwali crackers the morning after, start on the balcony, because that is where the residue blows indoors from. Get the order wrong and wet soot smears into a grey film on walls and grout that is much harder to shift. This guide on how to clean after Diwali crackers covers balconies, walls, windows, floors, fabrics and the smoky smell, step by step. Air quality in Delhi and much of north India is often poor in the days after the festival, so how to clean after Diwali crackers in a smoggy city also means keeping the fine dust down while you work.

What should you do before you start cleaning?

Check the air quality index for your city before you open windows or sweep outdoors. The Central Pollution Control Board publishes a daily AQI bulletin and live readings on its official website. When the reading is poor, keep outdoor sweeping short and do the indoor wet work first; air the house when the reading improves.

Wear rubber gloves and a well fitted N95 mask for sweeping ash and soot. Keep children, elderly family members, pets and anyone with asthma out of the room while you sweep. These air quality and home cleaning tips for Delhi apply all of November, not only the morning after.

1. Clear cracker debris from the balcony and terrace

Pick up the large pieces by hand with gloves on. Soak any cracker that did not go off, and any that looks only half burnt, in a bucket of water for a few hours before you bag it; never relight or open one. Sparkler wires are sharp and can stay hot for a while, so collect them in a metal tin. Bag the debris separately from kitchen waste and keep it out of drains, where paper and ash cause blockages.

2. How do you remove soot from walls without smearing it?

Remove soot dry first. Wipe the marks in one direction with a dry soot sponge (a rubber sponge sold for smoke stains) or a clean, dry microfibre cloth, turning it often. You can also vacuum the wall gently with the brush attachment. Water at this stage spreads the soot.

What comes next depends on the paint. Washable emulsion can then be wiped with a cloth wrung out in warm water and a few drops of dish soap, after you test a hidden patch. Distemper and lime wash do not tolerate scrubbing, so stop at the dry clean; a stubborn mark may need a paint touch up. Our guide to cleaning house walls explains how to tell the paint types apart.

3. Wash the balcony floor, railings and grills

Mist the floor lightly with water before you sweep, so the fine ash sticks together instead of rising. Sweep it into a dustpan, then scrub with a bucket of water and detergent. Black scorch marks on tiles usually lift with a paste of baking soda and water left for 15 minutes. Wipe railings and grills with a damp cloth, top to bottom, and dry metal parts so they do not rust.

4. Clean windows, mesh and sliding tracks

Smoke leaves a greasy haze on glass. Vacuum the mosquito mesh from the inside, then wipe it with a damp cloth. Clear the sliding tracks with a crevice nozzle, since ash collects there and blows back in each time the window moves. Wash the glass with warm water and dish soap and dry it with a lint free cloth. Do this on a cloudy afternoon or in shade, so the glass does not streak.

5. Clean indoor floors without stirring up dust

Dry sweeping lifts fine particles back into the air you breathe. Vacuum first if you have one, then damp mop. Use two buckets, one with cleaner and one with clean water to rinse the mop, so you are not spreading grey water around the house. On marble, use a pH neutral cleaner and skip vinegar and lemon, which etch the surface. For stone and tile care, read our guide to keeping floors clean and shiny.

6. Refresh curtains, sofa and carpets

Fabric traps both smoke and ash. Wash curtains and cushion covers on a normal cycle, and dry clean heavy or silk ones. Vacuum the sofa with the upholstery nozzle, then the carpet in two directions. For a smoky carpet, sprinkle baking soda, leave it overnight and vacuum it up. 7. Clean AC and air purifier filters

An AC that ran during the festival pulls smoke residue onto its filters. Switch it off at the mains, slide out the filters, rinse them under running water and let them dry completely in the shade before you refit them. Follow your air purifier manual: most pre filters can be vacuumed or rinsed, but the main filter usually cannot be washed and needs replacing on schedule. 8. How do you remove the smoky smell from the house?

Smell sits in fabric and on surfaces, so the steps above do most of the work. For what lingers, these are the most reliable ways to remove a bad smell from the house:

  • Cross ventilate for 15 to 20 minutes when the outdoor reading improves, with windows open on opposite sides
  • Leave open bowls of baking soda or activated charcoal in the living room and bedrooms for two or three days
  • Wipe hard surfaces and switchboards (with power off) using a cloth dampened with water and a little white vinegar, but not on marble
  • Clean the kitchen and bathroom exhaust fans, which hold smoky grease

To make the house smell good naturally, simmer a pan of water with cinnamon, cloves and orange or lemon peel for 20 minutes, keep fresh marigold or mogra in a bowl, and air washed linens indoors near a sunny window. On poor air days, avoid adding more smoke from incense, dhoop or camphor. Camphor and strong essential oils can also be harmful around babies, children and pets, so keep them out of reach.

Dust control tips for Delhi homes in the weeks after Diwali

Ash and fine dust keep blowing in well after the festival. A few post Diwali cleaning tips help keep the house clean between deep cleans:

  • Keep doormats outside and inside the main door and take shoes off at the entrance
  • Keep windows shut during the hours the AQI is worst and use the exhaust fan while cooking
  • Damp dust window sills, the TV unit and bedside tables every day, since a dry cloth only moves dust around
  • Change pillow covers and bedsheets weekly through November
  • Fit a door draught stopper on the balcony door to block the gap at the bottom

When should you call in professionals?

Book a professional clean if soot covers a large wall or a ceiling, if your walls are distemper and need careful handling, or if the whole house smells of smoke a week later. If a cracker caused a small fire indoors or on the balcony, have an electrician check the wiring and sockets nearby before you clean around them. Frequently Asked Questions

How soon after Diwali should I clean the balcony?

Clear loose cracker debris the next morning, especially any unburst crackers, which should be soaked in water before disposal. The deeper wash of the floor, walls and railings can wait a day or two until the air quality improves. Waiting too long lets soot bond to surfaces, which makes it harder to remove.

Can I use bleach to remove soot from balcony tiles?

Bleach is not needed for soot, which is mostly carbon and oil, and a detergent with a baking soda paste works better. If you do use bleach for mould or stains, dilute it as the label says, ventilate the area and never mix it with ammonia or acidic cleaners such as vinegar or toilet cleaner. Rinse the area with plain water afterwards.

Is it safe to open windows to air out the smoke after Diwali?

Open them when the outdoor reading has improved, and keep them shut when the AQI is at its worst. Short cross ventilation, 15 to 20 minutes with windows open on opposite sides, clears indoor smell without letting in too much outside dust. Check the CPCB bulletin or a reliable AQI app before you decide.

How do I get rangoli colour off the floor after Diwali?

Sweep or vacuum the dry powder first, since wetting it straight away can stain porous stone and grout. Then wipe with a damp cloth and mild detergent, working from the edge of the design inwards. On marble, use a pH neutral cleaner and avoid lemon or vinegar, which can dull the surface.

Do houseplants help with the smoky smell indoors?

Houseplants make a room feel fresher, but they do not clean smoke from indoor air at the speed you need after Diwali. Ventilation, washing fabrics, cleaning AC filters and an air purifier with a suitable filter do much more. Keep plants for how they look, and wipe their leaves, as soot dulls them too.
