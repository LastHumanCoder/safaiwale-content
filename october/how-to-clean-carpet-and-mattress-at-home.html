How to Clean Carpet and Mattress at Home: Step-by-Step Tips

The short answer to how to clean carpet at home is to vacuum slowly, treat stains one at a time, wash with as little water as possible and get the carpet dry within a day. Most of what goes wrong when people work out how to clean carpet at home comes from the last step, because a carpet that stays damp starts to smell and can grow mould. This guide covers how to clean carpet at home step by step, then the same for your mattress, plus pet stains, door mats and rugs. The drying advice at the end applies to everything here, so read it before you learn how to clean carpet at home with water.

Check the fibre and the weather first

Look at the label on the back of the carpet or the receipt. Synthetic carpets (nylon, polyester, polypropylene) tolerate mild detergent and lukewarm water. Wool needs cool water and a wool-safe or pH-neutral cleaner, because hot water and strong alkaline detergents can shrink it and make dyes run. Silk, viscose and hand-knotted rugs are best left to a professional.

Then check the weather. Any wet cleaning needs dry air and good airflow, so plan it for a sunny stretch rather than a humid monsoon week.

How to clean carpet at home, step by step

  1. Clear the floor. Move light furniture and put foil or plastic under the legs of anything heavy so wood stain or rust does not transfer onto damp fibres.
  2. Vacuum twice. Go slowly in one direction, then at right angles. Most visible dirt is dry grit, and wetting it first turns it into mud.
  3. Treat stains individually. Blot, never rub. Use cold water for blood, milk and egg; a drop of dish liquid in water for food and grease; and plain water for tea and coffee before anything stronger.
  4. Wash with foam, not water. Whisk a teaspoon of mild detergent (wool-safe for wool) into a litre of lukewarm water, apply the foam with a soft brush in small sections, and work in the direction of the pile.
  5. Rinse by blotting. Press a clean towel dampened with plain water over each section to lift the soap. Leftover detergent attracts dirt and makes the carpet look grubby again within weeks.
  6. Dry fast. Open windows, run fans and lift a corner of the carpet so air reaches the back.
  7. Groom the pile. Once dry, brush or vacuum against the pile so it stands up evenly.

How to clean mattress at home, step by step

A mattress collects sweat, skin flakes and dust mites, and unlike a carpet it can never be soaked. The method is mostly dry.

  1. Strip and wash the bedding on the hottest setting its care label allows.
  2. Vacuum every side with the upholstery tool, paying attention to seams and the tufted buttons where dust collects.
  3. Spot clean with foam. Use detergent foam on a white cloth for sweat marks and cold water for blood. Dab, do not pour, and blot with a dry towel straight away.
  4. Deodorise. Sieve a thin layer of baking soda over the top, leave it for several hours with a fan running, then vacuum it off.
  5. Air it. A few hours in sunlight or a breezy room helps the mattress dry and smell fresher. Protect foam mattresses from long periods of direct sun, which can break the foam down.
  6. Rotate it head to foot every few months, and flip it only if it is double-sided.
  7. Fit a washable mattress protector so the next spill reaches the cover, not the core.

Types of mattress cleaning methods by mattress

  • Memory foam and latex: vacuum, dry baking soda and minimal foam only. Foam holds water deep inside.
  • Spring mattresses: tolerate slightly more foam, but still blot dry.
  • Coir: keep it dry; vacuum and air it in sun and shade.
  • Cotton gaddas: sun them regularly and, when lumpy, have the cotton re-carded by a local cotton carder.

Pet stains and how to keep house clean with pets

For urine on a carpet or mattress, act fast. Blot up as much as you can with paper towels, pressing hard. Rinse with cold water and blot again. Then apply an enzyme cleaner made for pet stains, leave it for the time on the label so the enzymes can break down the odour, and blot dry. Skip ammonia-based cleaners: they smell like urine to a dog or cat and can draw the pet back to the same spot. For vomit, scrape solids first, then treat the stain the same way.

Day to day, these habits keep pet hair and odour under control:

  • Wipe paws with a damp cloth at the door after walks.
  • Wash pet bedding and blankets weekly.
  • Pull hair off rugs and sofas with a damp rubber glove before vacuuming.
  • Keep a washable throw on the bed if pets sleep there.

How to clean door mats and rugs

  • Coir door mats: shake or beat them outdoors weekly, scrub with a stiff brush, and hose them only occasionally. Dry them fully in the sun before putting them back.
  • Rubber-backed mats: hand wash or use a cold, gentle cycle and air dry. Hot water and tumble dryers crack the backing.
  • Cotton dhurries: test a corner for colour bleed, then hand wash or machine wash cold and dry flat in the shade.
  • Jute and sisal rugs: vacuum and blot spills dry. Water stains and browns plant fibres.

Why drying time matters: the mould warning

Damp fibres and padding are where mould starts. The US Environmental Protection Agency's guide to mould and moisture advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mould growth. At home, that means:

  • Aim to have a carpet dry within a day, and check the back and the padding, not just the surface.
  • Never lay a rug back on a damp floor or make a bed on a damp mattress.
  • Postpone wet cleaning during humid monsoon spells and use dry methods instead.
  • If you notice a musty smell or dark spots a few days later, the core stayed wet; call a professional before it spreads.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I wash a wool carpet with regular detergent?

It is better not to. Regular laundry detergent is often alkaline and can leave wool fibres rough, make dyes bleed and leave sticky residue. Use a cleaner labelled wool-safe or pH neutral, cool water and very little of it, and test a corner first. Valuable or hand-knotted wool rugs should go to a professional.

Does sunlight help freshen a mattress?

Yes, a few hours of sun helps a mattress dry out and lose stale odour, which is why sunning gaddas is a long Indian habit. Turn it halfway through so both sides get air. Limit direct sun on memory foam and latex, which can degrade with long exposure, and bring it in before evening dew sets in.

How often should carpets and mattresses be deep cleaned?

Vacuum carpets weekly and mattresses every month or two. A deep clean once a year suits most homes, and every six months suits homes with pets, small children or allergy sufferers. Clean sooner if you notice odour, visible soiling or a musty smell after the rains.

Will baking soda harm a memory foam mattress?

Dry baking soda is safe on most mattress covers, including memory foam, as long as you vacuum it off thoroughly afterwards. Never make it into a wet paste on foam, because the moisture soaks into the core. If your mattress has a removable cover, check its care label and wash the cover separately.

Can I remove bed bugs by cleaning my mattress myself?

Vacuuming seams and washing bedding hot can reduce numbers, but it will not clear an infestation, because bed bugs hide in bed frames, furniture joints and wall gaps as well as the mattress. If you see live bugs, shed skins or rusty spots on the sheets, book a pest control inspection. Cleaning works best after treatment, not instead of it.
