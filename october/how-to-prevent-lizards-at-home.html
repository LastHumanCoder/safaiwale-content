How to Prevent Lizards at Home: 12 Natural Ways That Work

The most reliable answer to how to prevent lizards at home is to close the gaps they come through and remove the insects they come in to eat. Repellents help, but how to prevent lizards at home starts with door seals, window mesh and a kitchen with fewer cockroaches, moths and flies. This guide covers how to prevent lizards at home in 12 natural ways: sealing entry points first, then cutting off food sources, then repellents such as pepper spray and camphor. Since several popular methods for how to prevent lizards at home use strong-smelling or toxic materials, we also explain which ones to keep away from babies and pets.

Why do lizards come into your home?

The lizards on Indian walls are almost always house geckos. They are not venomous and rarely bite, but they leave droppings on walls and shelves, and like many reptiles they can carry Salmonella bacteria, so you do not want them near food. They come indoors for three things: insects to eat, warm hiding spots, and an easy way in.

That last point changes how you deal with them. A gecko on your wall near a tube light is there because insects gather around the light. Take away the insects and the gaps, and the lizards have little reason to stay.

How to pest proof your home: ways 1 to 4

Geckos can squeeze through very small gaps, so these steps are about closing the routes they actually use.

1. Seal the gap under doors

Fit a rubber door seal or brush strip under the main door and the balcony door. The same seal keeps out cockroaches and dust.

2. Put mesh on windows, ventilators and exhaust openings

Fine mesh on windows and bathroom ventilators blocks lizards and the insects they follow. Kitchen exhaust fan openings are a common route when the fan is off, so fit a mesh cover or a flap shutter.

3. Close holes around pipes and cables

Look at where the AC pipe, drain pipes, gas line and TV or internet cables enter the wall. Fill those holes with sealant, putty or white cement, leaving gas line fittings as your gas supplier advises. Check the gap around the AC unit's wall sleeve too.

4. Trim plants and clear the balcony

Creepers and plants touching walls and window grills act as ladders, so trim them back. Clear stacked pots, old boxes and unused furniture from the balcony, as these give lizards a resting place right next to your windows.

How do you cut off a lizard's food supply: ways 5 to 8?

A home with few insects cannot feed many lizards. These steps make your home a poor hunting ground.

5. Control the insects indoors

Cockroaches, ants, moths and flies are all lizard food. Keep counters clean, food sealed and bins lidded, and deal with any cockroach activity quickly. Our tips to prevent cockroach infestation at your home cover the kitchen routine in detail.

6. Change how you light the house at night

Bright white lights near windows and on balconies pull in moths and other flying insects, and geckos wait beside them. Switch off balcony and outdoor lights when you do not need them, use warm yellow bulbs outdoors, which draw fewer insects, and draw curtains after dark so indoor light does not attract insects to the windows.

7. Clear clutter behind frames and furniture

Lizards rest in dark, undisturbed gaps: behind wall frames, wardrobes, calendars, AC units and stacks of cartons. Move frames and furniture once a month, wipe behind them, and get rid of stored cardboard. A deep cleaning service can clear the spots you rarely reach, such as the tops of cupboards.

8. Fix damp corners and leaks

Damp bathrooms, leaking pipes and wet corners draw insects and give lizards water. Fix leaks, keep bathroom windows open after use, and dry out damp storerooms. The monsoon pest control guide explains why insect and lizard activity rises during the rains.

Which natural repellents keep lizards away: ways 9 to 12?

Repellents make a spot less pleasant for a lizard. They work best once entry points and food sources are handled, and they need refreshing every few days.

9. A DIY pest repellent spray with pepper

Stir a teaspoon of black pepper or red chilli powder into a spray bottle of water and spray behind cupboards, around window frames and in corners where lizards rest. Test on a hidden patch first, since it can mark light paint. This spray irritates eyes and skin, so wear gloves, keep it away from your face and do not use it where children play or pets lie.

10. Onion and garlic near entry points

Slices of onion or crushed garlic cloves placed near windows and doors give off a strong smell that some lizards avoid. Replace them every two or three days before they rot, or they will draw flies instead.

11. Camphor in closed corners

Camphor tablets placed inside cupboards, behind frames or in storeroom corners give off a smell that may keep lizards away from those spots. The effect is local and fades as the camphor evaporates. Camphor is toxic if swallowed, so read the safety section below before you use it.

12. Move lizards out gently

If a lizard is already inside, switch off the lights, open the nearest window or door and guide it out with a broom. You can also cover it with a box, slide a card underneath and release it outside. This is quicker than any spray and does no harm to the lizard.

What pest control precautions should you take with babies and pets?

Many home remedies are strong chemicals in disguise. These precautions apply to everything above.

  • Camphor: even a small amount can be dangerous if a child swallows it. Keep tablets in closed containers or high cupboards, never on the floor or in a baby's room, and do not burn camphor in a closed room where a baby sleeps.
  • Naphthalene: keep mothballs out of homes with babies. Newborns are especially sensitive to naphthalene, and the vapours collect in closed wardrobes and rooms.
  • Strong essential oils: eucalyptus, peppermint, clove and similar oils can irritate a baby's breathing and can be harmful to pets, especially cats. Do not apply them near a child's face or run a diffuser in a nursery.
  • Pepper and chilli spray: keep it away from eyes, cots, toys and pet bedding.
  • Any insecticide: follow the label on dose, ventilation and how long to keep children and pets out of the room.

If a child or pet swallows camphor, a mothball or any repellent, call a doctor or vet straight away, even if they seem fine. Our guide to eco-friendly pest control for homes lists gentler options for family homes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are house lizards dangerous to my family?

House geckos are not venomous and rarely bite, so they pose little direct danger. The main concern is hygiene: their droppings land on walls and shelves, and like many reptiles they can carry Salmonella bacteria. Wipe surfaces where droppings fall, keep food covered and wash your hands after cleaning up.

Why do I see more lizards in summer and the monsoon?

Geckos are more active in warm weather, and the monsoon brings a surge of insects that they hunt. Damp walls and open windows also give them more ways in. That is when insect control and window mesh matter most.

How often should I replace natural lizard repellents?

Onion and garlic slices need replacing every two or three days before they rot. Pepper spray should be reapplied every few days, and after you mop or wipe the area. Camphor tablets shrink as they evaporate, so replace them when they become small, and keep them in closed spots out of children's reach.

Can a lizard fall into food and make it unsafe?

It can happen, especially in kitchens where lizards rest above the stove or shelves. If a lizard or its droppings fall into food, throw the food away rather than picking it out, and wash the vessel well. Keep pots covered while cooking and storing food.

Do ultrasonic pest repellers work on lizards?

We have not seen ultrasonic plug-in devices reliably keep geckos away in homes. Lizards follow food and shelter, so a device does little while insects and gaps remain. Spend the money on door seals, window mesh and insect control instead, which address the reasons lizards come in.
