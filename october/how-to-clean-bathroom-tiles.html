How to Clean Bathroom Tiles: 12 Easy Ways to Remove Stains

The quickest answer to how to clean bathroom tiles is to name the stain first, because white hard water spots, grey soap scum, dark grout and black mould each need a different cleaner. Below we show how to clean bathroom tiles in 12 stain-by-stain steps, using a vinegar spray, a baking soda paste and a few labelled products from the local store. Before you learn how to clean bathroom tiles with acids or bleach, read the safety box on what never to mix. And if your floor is marble or granite, how to clean bathroom tiles changes completely, so check that section too.

Check your tile type and kit first

Most Indian bathrooms use glazed ceramic or vitrified tiles on the walls and anti-skid ceramic on the floor. These handle mild acids like vinegar well. Marble, granite, kota and other natural stone do not: acids etch them, leaving dull patches that cleaning cannot fix. If you are unsure, put a drop of vinegar on a hidden corner; if it fizzes or the spot turns dull, treat that surface as stone and use only pH-neutral cleaners.

Keep this kit ready: rubber gloves, a stiff grout brush or old toothbrush, a non-scratch scrub pad, a squeegee, two microfibre cloths, a spray bottle and a bucket. Open the window and switch on the exhaust fan before you start.

Which bathroom cleaners should you never mix?

Never mix these

  • Bleach and any acid (vinegar, lemon juice, or acid toilet and tile cleaners). This releases chlorine gas.
  • Bleach and ammonia, found in some glass cleaners. This releases chloramine vapour.
  • Bleach and rubbing alcohol. This forms chloroform and other harmful compounds.
  • Hydrogen peroxide and vinegar in one bottle. The mixture is corrosive to skin and eyes.
  • Two different drain or toilet cleaners one after the other.

Use one product, rinse the surface with plain water, and only then use another. If your eyes burn or you start coughing, leave the bathroom, get fresh air and seek medical help if it does not settle.

DIY bathroom cleaner recipe

Three home mixes cover most stains. Label each bottle clearly and never pour one into another.

  • Vinegar spray: equal parts white vinegar and water. For glazed ceramic, vitrified tiles, glass and chrome only.
  • Grout paste: three spoons of baking soda to one spoon of water, plus a few drops of dish liquid.
  • Everyday spray: a teaspoon of dish liquid in 500 ml of warm water. Safe on every surface, including stone.

12 easy ways to clean bathroom tiles, stain by stain

1. Squeegee after every bath

Hard water spots and soap scum form when water dries on the tile. A thirty-second pass with a squeegee on the shower wall and glass removes most of that water, so the deep cleans below are needed far less often.

2. Weekly wash for everyday grime

Spray the everyday mix on walls, then floors, working from the top down so dirty drips land on areas you have not cleaned yet. Scrub floor tiles with a stiff brush, since anti-skid texture traps dirt that a mop slides over. Rinse and dry with a cloth.

3. Hard water spots on wall tiles

Spray the vinegar mix, leave it for 10 to 15 minutes so the acid can soften the minerals, scrub with the non-scratch pad and rinse well. Repeat on stubborn patches rather than reaching for steel wool.

4. Thick white scale near the tap and shower

Homes on borewell or tanker water often get crusty white build-up. Soak paper towels in warm undiluted vinegar, press them onto the scale, leave them for 30 to 60 minutes, then scrub and rinse. For very heavy scale on glazed tile, use a descaler labelled for tiles and follow its dwell time exactly.

5. How to remove hard water stains from taps

Wrap chrome taps in a cloth soaked in the vinegar mix for 20 to 30 minutes, then scrub the base and spout with an old toothbrush, rinse and buff dry. Gold, brass, matte black and other coated finishes can be damaged by acid, so check the maker's care instructions and use only the everyday spray on them.

6. Clogged showerhead

Fill a plastic bag with the vinegar mix, tie it around a chrome showerhead so the nozzles sit in the liquid, and leave it for an hour. Remove it, rub the rubber nozzles with your thumb to push out loosened scale, and run hot water through for a minute.

7. Soap scum film

Soap scum is a greasy grey layer, so it needs a mild alkali rather than acid. Spread the grout paste over the film, leave it for 10 minutes, scrub with the pad and rinse thoroughly. If white mineral spots remain once it is clean and rinsed, follow with the vinegar spray as a separate step.

8. How to clean grout between tiles

Spread the grout paste along the lines, wait 10 minutes and scrub along the length of each line with a grout brush. Rinse with a sponge, changing the water often so you are not wiping dirt back in.

9. Deeply stained grout

For grout that stays grey after way 8, dissolve oxygen bleach (sodium percarbonate) powder in warm water as the pack directs, apply it to the lines, leave it for the time on the label and scrub. Oxygen bleach is gentler on coloured grout than chlorine bleach. Test a small section first.

10. Black mould on grout and silicone

Ventilate the room and wear gloves. Apply a chlorine bleach solution diluted as the bottle directs, or 3% hydrogen peroxide, to white grout and silicone, leave it for 10 to 15 minutes, scrub and rinse. If the black spots sit under the silicone, no cleaner will reach them; cut the old bead out and reseal the joint.

11. Rust stains

Rust rings come from iron buckets, steel stands and old razor or spray cans. Rub light marks with half a lemon dipped in salt, wait 10 minutes and rinse. For heavy rust on glazed tile, use a rust remover labelled for tiles, with gloves and the window open, and rinse until no residue remains. Keep both off marble and stone, and swap metal stands for plastic ones.

12. How to clean bathroom exhaust fan

Switch the fan off at the switch and at the MCB, and wait for the blades to stop. Remove the grille, wash it in warm soapy water, and wipe the blades with a barely damp cloth so no water reaches the motor. Vacuum dust from the housing, let everything dry completely and refit. A clean fan dries the room faster after a bath, which slows the return of mould in grout.

Bathroom cleaning mistakes that damage tiles

  • Using toilet acid on wall tiles. It eats into cement grout and dulls chrome over time.
  • Vinegar or lemon on marble. Acid etches stone permanently.
  • Steel wool or wire brushes. They scratch glazed tiles, glass and taps, and the scratches hold dirt.
  • Letting a cleaner dry on the surface. Residue attracts dirt and can leave its own white film.
  • One cloth for the toilet and the basin. Keep separate, colour-coded cloths.
  • Cleaning with the door shut. Fumes build up quickly in a small bathroom.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use vinegar on marble or granite bathroom floors?

No. Vinegar, lemon juice and acid toilet cleaners etch marble, kota and many granites, leaving dull, rough patches that only polishing can remove. Clean stone with warm water and a few drops of dish liquid or a cleaner labelled pH neutral, and dry it after cleaning to limit water spots.

How often should I deep clean bathroom tiles?

With a daily squeegee and a weekly wash, a deep clean of grout, scale and silicone every month or two is enough for most homes. Bathrooms on hard borewell water or with poor ventilation may need it more often. If mould returns within days of cleaning, improve airflow before you clean again.

Why does my grout turn black again so quickly?

Grout is porous, so it holds moisture and soap residue that mould feeds on. Poor ventilation, a weak exhaust fan and water left standing on the floor speed this up. Once the grout is clean and fully dry, a grout sealer from a hardware store slows the problem, and should be reapplied as the pack directs.

Is it safe to use toilet cleaner on bathroom tiles?

Most strong toilet cleaners sold in India contain hydrochloric acid, which is meant for the toilet bowl only. On walls and floors it wears away cement grout, can damage chrome fittings and will etch marble. Use the everyday spray or a dedicated tile cleaner instead, and never combine an acid cleaner with bleach.

How do I keep glass shower screens free of water spots?

Squeegee the glass after every shower, top to bottom, and wipe the bottom edge with a cloth. Once a week, spray the vinegar mix, leave it for five minutes, wipe and dry with a microfibre cloth. If spots have built up for months, repeat the vinegar step a few times rather than scraping.
