How to Clean Water Tank at Home After Monsoon: Full Guide

Here is how to clean water tank at home in one line: shut the inlet, drain the tank, scoop out the sludge, scrub every wall with a stiff nylon brush, disinfect with chlorine measured to the label, then rinse, flush and refill. That routine is how to clean water tank at home safely when the tank is a rooftop plastic one you can reach from outside. Knowing how to clean water tank at home also means knowing when to stop: underground sumps and deep concrete tanks are confined spaces, and we explain below where DIY ends. Once you have worked out how to clean water tank at home, use the same dry weekend after the monsoon for rusty window grills, slippery balcony tiles and damp patches on the walls, which this guide covers too.

Why clean the water tank after the monsoon?

During the rains, the supply that fills your tank often carries more silt than usual, and it settles as a soft brown layer on the tank floor. Loose lids let in rainwater, leaves and insects, and humid weeks feed the slime on the inner walls.

The mosquito angle matters too. Aedes mosquitoes, which spread dengue, breed in clean stored water, and an overhead tank with a loose lid or a torn overflow mesh is an ideal spot for them. Our older post on housekeeping habits that help prevent dengue covers the rest of the home.

How often should you do it? Clean it whenever the water looks cloudy, smells, or leaves grit in the bucket, and make two cleans a fixed habit: one after the monsoon and one before peak summer, when the tank works hardest. Borewell homes and old concrete tanks need it more often. Our earlier article on why your overhead tank needs cleaning more often than you think makes the full case; this guide stays on the method.

What do you need, and what are the safety rules?

Gather everything before you drain the tank.

  • Two buckets and a mug for scooping sludge
  • A stiff nylon brush on a long handle, plus an old toothbrush for corners and fittings
  • Rubber gloves, rubber boots and eye protection
  • Bleaching powder or liquid sodium hypochlorite, with the pack's dosage instructions
  • A torch, a stable ladder and a second person
  • Clean cloths and, if you have one, a wet-dry vacuum

Safety rules that are not optional

  • A tank is a confined space. Fumes build up fast inside. Never lean in or enter alone, keep the lid fully open, and keep a helper outside who can see and hear you.
  • Do not enter underground sumps or tanks deeper than your arm can reach. Low oxygen and trapped gases in sumps can overcome a person quickly. Book a professional crew.
  • Never mix bleach with acid or ammonia cleaners (toilet cleaners, descalers, some glass sprays). The mix releases toxic gas.
  • Switch off the pump at the mains before you start, and keep extension leads well away from water.
  • Do not use detergent, phenyl or floor cleaner inside a drinking water tank. They leave residue and taste in the water.

How to clean water tank at home: 9 steps

These steps are for a rooftop plastic or steel tank of the size most homes have, cleaned from outside through the lid.

1. Store water and tell the household

Fill a few buckets for drinking and cooking. Expect the tank to be out of use for half a day.

2. Shut the inlet and switch off the motor

Close the inlet valve so no fresh water enters while you work, and switch off the pump at the mains.

3. Drain the tank

Use the water for washing floors and balconies, or open the washout valve. Do not send the last few inches through your taps, since that sludge clogs aerators and geyser inlets.

4. Remove the sludge

Scoop the settled mud into a bucket with a mug, or use a wet-dry vacuum to lift the fine silt a mug leaves behind.

5. Scrub the walls, floor and lid

Work top to bottom with the nylon brush and clean water, paying attention to the waterline ring, corners and outlet. Use the toothbrush on the float valve. Avoid wire brushes on plastic, since scratches give slime a grip.

6. Rinse and drain again

Splash clean water down the walls and remove it the same way as the sludge, until it runs clear.

7. Disinfect

Mix bleaching powder or sodium hypochlorite exactly as the pack directs for disinfecting stored water or tanks, wipe or spray it onto the inner walls, and leave it for the contact time on the label. If you dose a partly filled tank instead, run each tap until you can smell chlorine, then let it stand before flushing.

8. Flush the lines

Drain the solution, refill a little, and run every tap until the strong chlorine smell fades.

9. Check the lid, vent and overflow

Before the final refill, make sure the lid closes tightly, the vent and overflow pipes have intact mesh, and the tank body has no cracks. A tight lid keeps out mosquitoes and dust.

How to clean window grills after the monsoon

Monsoon leaves grills coated in road dust and rain splash, and starts rust at the welds of iron grills. Here is how to clean window grills properly.

  1. Dry brush first. Use a soft paintbrush or a dry cloth to knock off loose dust and cobwebs.
  2. Wash with mild soapy water. Wrap a cloth around each bar and slide it along; a toothbrush reaches the scroll work.
  3. Treat rust spots. Rub surface rust with fine steel wool or sandpaper, wipe off the powder, and touch up with a rust-inhibiting primer and matching paint once dry.
  4. Dry it. Wipe the grill dry and clean the sill and window channel underneath.

Invisible grills (steel cables) only need a damp microfibre cloth run along each wire. Skip acidic cleaners and abrasive pads, which can damage the coating. See our post on invisible safety grills.

How to clean balcony tiles

A balcony that stayed wet for weeks often has green film along the grout and pigeon droppings in the corners. This is how to clean balcony tiles without damaging them.

  1. Clear the drain outlet. Pull out leaves and silt from the floor trap first, so the wash water can leave.
  2. Deal with droppings carefully. Wear gloves and a mask, spray the droppings with water so they do not turn to dust, then scrape them off.
  3. Scrub the algae. On glazed ceramic or vitrified tiles, a diluted bleach solution and a stiff deck brush lift the green film. Rinse well. On natural stone such as Kota, marble or granite, use a mild pH-neutral cleaner, since bleach and acid can etch and stain stone.
  4. Do the grout lines. Use a grout brush along the joints and rinse. Note cracked grout for repair.
  5. Let it dry fully and raise plant pots on stands so water does not pool.

If pigeons keep returning, a net is the lasting fix. Our guide to getting rid of pigeons from balconies and roofs explains the options.

How to prevent dampness in walls after the rains

Damp patches and bubbling paint in October mean water got into the wall during the monsoon. Learning how to prevent dampness in walls starts with finding the source.

Find where the water comes from

  • Seepage from outside: patches on an outer wall or under a terrace, worse after heavy rain.
  • Plumbing leaks: a damp patch that stays the same in dry weather.
  • Rising damp: a tide line low on ground floor walls.
  • Condensation: mould in corners, behind wardrobes and on cold walls in rooms with little airflow.

Clean and dry the patch

Wear gloves and a mask, test any cleaner on a hidden spot, and wipe the mould with a mould remover or a mild diluted bleach solution on washable paint. Let the wall dry fully before repainting, because paint over damp plaster blisters again within weeks.

Stop it coming back

  • Clear terrace outlets, gutters and downpipes so rain does not pond against walls. Our gutter maintenance tips show what to check.
  • Seal cracks in external plaster and around window frames before next season.
  • Keep furniture off outer walls and run exhaust fans in wet rooms.
  • Fix plumbing leaks promptly, and call a waterproofing contractor for seepage that returns every year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use detergent or phenyl to clean my drinking water tank?

No. Detergent, phenyl and floor cleaners leave a film and taste that is hard to rinse out of a tank. Scrub with plain water and a nylon brush, then disinfect with bleaching powder or sodium hypochlorite dosed to the label, and flush the taps until the strong smell goes.

How long after cleaning can we drink the tank water?

Wait until you have flushed every tap and the strong chlorine smell has faded, which usually takes a few hours after the final refill. A faint chlorine smell for a day is normal. A clean tank does not replace your RO or boiling for drinking water.

Is it safe to clean an underground sump myself?

We advise against it. Underground sumps can hold low oxygen and trapped gases that overcome a person quickly,. A professional crew uses pumps and long-handled tools to clean without anyone entering unprotected, which is the safer choice.

What causes a green film inside a plastic water tank?

Green film is algae, which grows where sunlight gets through a thin or light-coloured tank wall, or through a gap at the lid. Scrub it off, disinfect, and then reduce light entry by keeping the lid tight and, for thin tanks, shading the tank or replacing it with a thicker, darker one rated for potable water.

Do I need to clean the tank if I have an RO purifier?

Yes. The RO treats only the water that goes through it, while your bathrooms, kitchen sink, washing machine and geyser all draw straight from the tank. Sludge in the tank also loads the RO pre-filters faster, so a clean tank helps the purifier filters last longer.
