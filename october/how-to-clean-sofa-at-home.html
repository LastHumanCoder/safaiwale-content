How to Clean Sofa at Home: 9 Methods for Every Fabric Type

The answer to how to clean sofa at home starts with a small tag under the seat cushion, because it tells you whether water, solvent, both or neither is safe on your fabric. Once you know that code, how to clean sofa at home comes down to matching the method to the material. Below are 9 methods that cover cotton, linen, velvet, microfibre, leather, faux leather, suede, jute and blends, each showing how to clean sofa at home with things you probably already own. If you remember one rule about how to clean sofa at home, make it this: use the least moisture that gets the job done.

What do the W, S, WS and X codes on your sofa mean?

Most branded sofas carry a cleaning code on a label under the cushions or on the base. Check it before you touch a stain, because it decides which methods below are safe.

CodeWhat it meansFabrics that often carry it
WWater-based cleaners are fine. Mild detergent foam, then blot.Cotton, polyester, many blends
SSolvent only. No water, which leaves rings or shrinks the fibre.Microfibre, linen, viscose, some velvets
WS or SWEither water or solvent cleaners, used sparingly.Cotton-polyester and linen blends
XVacuum and soft brush only. No liquids of any kind at home.Jute, some natural weaves, delicate silks

Many local and custom-made sofas in India have no tag at all. In that case, treat the sofa as the most delicate fibre you suspect it contains, run the patch test in the next section, and start with Method 1 before you try anything wet.

Best sofa cleaner for home use: what to keep in the cupboard

For most types of sofa fabric cleaning, this short kit covers everything:

  • A vacuum with an upholstery nozzle and a crevice tool.
  • A colourless, mild liquid detergent or dish soap (coloured soaps can tint pale fabric).
  • Rubbing alcohol (70% isopropyl) in a spray bottle, for S-coded fabrics.
  • Baking soda and cornflour, for odours and fresh oil stains.
  • White microfibre cloths and a white sponge. White matters, because dyed cloths can bleed onto the sofa.
  • A soft clothes brush, plus a suede brush and suede eraser if you own suede.
  • A leather cleaner and conditioner if your sofa is real leather.

Rubbing alcohol is flammable, so keep it away from diyas, candles and the gas stove, especially around Diwali.

How to patch test before you clean

A patch test takes ten minutes and saves you from a permanent watermark on a cushion.

  1. Pick a hidden spot: the back of the sofa near the floor, or the underside of a seat cushion.
  2. Apply a small amount of the exact solution you plan to use, with the same cloth.
  3. Blot gently, then leave it to dry fully, ideally with a fan running.
  4. Check for colour on the white cloth, a ring, stiffness, shrinkage or a change in sheen.
  5. If anything changed, stop and move to a gentler method or call a professional.

9 methods for every fabric type

Method 1: Dry vacuum and brush (every sofa, and the only method for X codes)

Remove the cushions and vacuum the deck, the seams and the gap where the back meets the seat where crumbs and hair collect. Vacuum both sides of each cushion slowly. On fabric sofas, sprinkle baking soda, leave it for 30 to 60 minutes to absorb odour, then vacuum it out. For X-coded jute or delicate weaves, stop here and brush loose dirt away with a soft brush.

Method 2: Cotton

Cotton usually carries a W code and handles water well. Whisk half a teaspoon of colourless detergent into a mug of lukewarm water until it foams, and apply only the foam with a white cloth. Blot from the outside of the stain inwards so it does not spread, then blot again with a cloth dampened in plain water to lift the soap residue. If the covers zip off, check the care label first; cotton can shrink in a hot wash or dryer, so use a cold, gentle cycle and dry in the shade.

Method 3: Linen

Linen marks easily and often carries an S or WS code. For a WS sofa, use a barely damp cloth with the detergent foam and work the moisture out towards the nearest seam so you do not leave a hard edge. For S-coded linen, use a solvent spot cleaner made for dry-clean-only fabric, applied to the cloth rather than the sofa.

Method 4: Velvet

Vacuum velvet with the brush attachment in the direction of the pile. Polyester velvet usually tolerates a little detergent foam; cotton velvet watermarks and crushes, so treat it as S unless the tag says otherwise. To lift crushed pile, hold a garment steamer a few centimetres away without touching the fabric, then brush the pile gently once it is dry.

Method 5: Microfibre

Microfibre is usually S-coded, and rubbing alcohol is the standard home fix. Mist the stained area lightly, scrub with a white sponge, and let it evaporate. Alcohol dries quickly, so it leaves far fewer rings than water. Once dry, brush the area with a soft brush in circles to bring the nap back.

Method 6: Leather

Dust first with a dry microfibre cloth. Then wipe with a cloth wrung out in water with a few drops of mild, pH-neutral soap, follow with a cloth dampened in clean water, and dry immediately. Condition the leather with a leather conditioner a few times a year so it does not crack. On unfinished (aniline) leather, which darkens when you touch it with a wet finger, use the conditioner maker's cleaner only and test first.

Method 7: Faux leather (PU and rexine)

Faux leather is plastic-coated fabric, so mild soapy water and a soft cloth are all it needs. Wipe, rinse with a clean damp cloth and dry. Keep alcohol, nail polish remover and solvent cleaners well away, because they strip the coating and cause the peeling you often see on older PU sofas.

Method 8: Suede

Real suede is a dry-clean fabric at home. Brush it in one direction with a suede brush to lift dust, rub light marks with a suede eraser, and cover a fresh oil stain with cornflour for a few hours before brushing it away. Do not apply water. Microsuede is a synthetic and follows Method 5 instead.

Method 9: Jute and blends

Jute and other natural plant-fibre weaves brown and stain when wet, so blot any spill at once with a dry cloth and stick to Method 1. For blends, the most delicate fibre in the mix decides the method: a cotton-polyester blend can take detergent foam, while anything with viscose or linen in it should be handled as S-coded.

Sofa cleaning mistakes to avoid

  • Rubbing a stain. Rubbing pushes it deeper and frays the fibre. Blot, lift, repeat.
  • Soaking the cushion. Water that reaches the foam takes days to dry and can smell musty, especially in the monsoon.
  • Hot water on milk, blood or egg. Heat sets protein stains. Use cold water first.
  • Mixing cleaners. Never combine bleach with ammonia or acid-based products; the fumes are dangerous in a closed room.
  • A hairdryer on high heat. It shrinks fabric and cracks leather. Use a fan and open windows.
  • Sitting on it too soon. A damp seat picks up dirt from clothes and flattens the pile.

A sofa cleaning checklist by season

A simple routine stops stains reaching the point where home methods fail:

  • Every week: vacuum the seats and crevices, then flip and rotate loose cushions so they wear evenly.
  • Every month: wipe leather and faux leather, brush velvet and suede, and spot-check armrests where hands and hair oil build up.
  • Before the monsoon: keep the sofa a few inches off damp walls and run a fan in closed rooms.
  • Before Diwali: vacuum thoroughly, treat old marks with the right method above, and put washable throws on the seats guests use most.
  • Once a year or so: book a professional deep clean, which removes the body oils and dust that build up inside the foam.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a steam cleaner on my sofa at home?

Only on W or WS fabrics that the patch test shows can take heat and moisture, such as most polyester and cotton. Keep the nozzle moving, do not let water soak into the foam, and never steam leather, suede, linen or jute. For a full steam or extraction clean, a professional machine pulls the moisture back out, which a handheld steamer cannot do.

How long does a sofa take to dry after cleaning?

A spot clean with foam or alcohol usually dries within a few hours with a fan running. A wetter clean of a whole sofa can take most of a day, and longer in humid weather. Open the windows, run a fan and keep people off the seats until the fabric feels completely dry to the touch.

How do I remove a turmeric or curry stain from a fabric sofa?

Scrape off any solid food with a blunt knife, then blot with the detergent foam from Method 2 on W-coded fabric. Turmeric is stubborn, but its colour fades in sunlight, so a removable cotton cover can go in the sun for an hour after washing. Avoid chlorine bleach on coloured fabric, because it will leave a pale patch.

How often should a sofa be professionally cleaned?

For a sofa in daily family use, once a year is a sensible default. Homes with pets, small children or allergy sufferers often book every six months.

What should I do if my sofa has no cleaning tag?

Assume it needs the gentlest treatment until you know more. Vacuum it, then patch test detergent foam on a hidden spot and wait for it to dry before using it anywhere visible. If you see a ring, colour transfer or stiffness, switch to dry methods and ask a professional to identify the fabric.
