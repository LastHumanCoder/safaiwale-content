How to Declutter Your Home Before Diwali: 15 Easy Ideas

The simplest answer to how to declutter your home before Diwali: work one room at a time, empty one storage area completely, sort every item into keep, donate or discard, and only then clean the empty shelf. If you have put off learning how to declutter your home because the job looks endless, the 15 ideas below break it into small, room by room tasks that suit Indian flats with lofts, box beds and steel almirahs. Most take 30 to 60 minutes each. Knowing how to declutter your home before the festival also shortens the deep clean that follows, because nobody has to dust, wash or polish things you were going to give away. We have ordered this guide on how to declutter your home from the entrance inwards, the way a guest sees it.

When should you start, and what do you need?

Start three to four weeks before Diwali, which in 2026 means mid October, since Diwali falls on Sunday 8 November and Dhanteras on Friday 6 November. Decluttering first is the order that saves effort, and it sits at the top of every list of Diwali decluttering tips for a reason: clear the shelf, then clean it once.

Before you open the first cupboard, keep three large bags or cartons ready and label them KEEP ELSEWHERE, DONATE and DISCARD. Add a fourth for newspapers and scrap metal, which the kabadiwala buys by weight. Set a 30 minute timer per zone. Stopping when it rings is one of the most practical decluttering tips for Indian homes with full lofts and storage beds.

Entrance and living room: ideas 1 to 4

1. Cut the shoe pile down to this month's shoes

Keep only the pairs each person wore in the last month on the rack by the door. Box the wedding shoes and winter boots on a higher shelf, throw out broken chappals, and donate outgrown children's school shoes while they are still wearable.

2. Give keys, bills and bags one landing spot

A small tray or two wall hooks near the door ends the daily search for keys. Go through the paper pile here: takeaway menus, old warranty cards, paid bills older than a year. File what you need, and put the rest in the paper bag for recycling.

3. Clear the coffee table and TV unit

Use a simple test: only things used every day stay on an open surface. Remotes, a coaster set and one decorative piece can stay. Old magazines, newspaper stacks, spare chargers and loose medicines go to their proper homes.

4. Edit the glass display cabinet

Take everything out, wipe the glass and shelves, and put back only what you like looking at. Empty Diwali sweet and dry fruit gift boxes are the usual culprits; keep two or three sturdy ones for storage and send the rest to recycling.

Kitchen: ideas 5 to 8

5. Retire duplicate utensils before Dhanteras shopping

If your family buys new steel, brass or silver items on Dhanteras, make room before you shop. Count your kadhais, pressure cookers and serving bowls, keep the ones you use, and donate working duplicates. Dented or broken steel can go to the kabadiwala as scrap. Throw out chipped crockery, because cracks hold grime that no amount of washing removes.

6. Check every masala, dal and dry fruit tin

Open each jar. Discard anything past its date, anything with tiny moths, webbing or weevils, and spices that have lost their smell. Crumbs and open packets in the back of a cabinet are what draw cockroaches indoors, and our guide on how to prevent a cockroach infestation at home explains why a tidy pantry matters as much as any spray.

7. Shrink the plastic box and polythene bag stash

Match every container to a lid; any box without a lid, or lid without a box, goes. Keep a fixed number of carry bags folded inside one bag, and recycle the rest.

8. Clear the counter and the fridge

Appliances you use less than once a week (the sandwich maker, the second mixer jar set) move into a cabinet. In the fridge, discard expired sauces, mouldy pickles and leftovers older than two days, then wipe the shelves with warm water and a mild detergent. Bedrooms and wardrobes: ideas 9 to 11

9. Sort the wardrobe with the one year test

Take out one shelf at a time. If you have not worn it since last Diwali, it goes to the donate pile unless it is festive or wedding wear you genuinely plan to use. Wearable clothes can go to a local clothes donation drive or a neighbourhood collection point. Torn cotton makes good dusters and mopping cloths, so cut a few before throwing the rest out.

10. Empty the box bed and the under bed space

Box beds collect old quilts, spare bedsheets and suitcases nobody has opened since the last move. Pull it all out. Air razais in the sun for a few hours, and check the corners for silverfish, cockroach droppings or small dark spots on seams that can mean bed bugs. 11. Clear the bedside and dressing table

Sort old medicines here. Do not flush them or throw loose tablets into the open bin where children or animals can reach them; ask your chemist whether they take back expired medicines, and if not, seal them in their original strip inside a bag before disposal.

Bathroom and kids room: ideas 12 and 13

12. Empty the bathroom cabinet of half used bottles

Combine two half bottles of the same shampoo, throw out expired sunscreen, rusty razors and hair ties, and keep only one spare of each toiletry. With the shelves bare, it is the right moment to descale taps and scrub grout, and our bathroom deep cleaning guide covers that step by step.

13. Rotate toys and school books with your children

Let children choose; they keep what they decide. Broken toys and puzzles with missing pieces go. Good textbooks and storybooks can go to younger cousins or a school book bank.

Loft, balcony and old electronics: ideas 14 and 15

14. Bring down the loft and clear the balcony

Lofts and balconies hold the things nobody wants to decide on: empty cartons, dried paint tins, a broken cooler, an old cot. Cartons are a favourite hiding place for cockroaches and silverfish, so flatten them and send them out with the raddi. Use a step ladder, never a chair. For home organization ideas in a small apartment, swap open loft piles for labelled boxes of one size so you can see what is up there without climbing.

15. Collect dead electronics, cables and batteries

Old phones, chargers, earphones, remote controls and dead batteries should not go in the household bin. Factory reset phones and remove the SIM and memory card, then hand them to an authorised e-waste collector or a brand take-back point. Keep used batteries in a closed box until you drop them off.

How do you stay clutter free after Diwali?

Diwali brings a wave of new things into the house, from gift hampers to new utensils, so set a one in, one out rule for the festive week: a new bedsheet set in means an old one out. For how to maintain a clean home daily, a 10 minute reset each evening works better than a big weekend clean. Put everything on open surfaces back in its place, wash whatever is in the sink, and empty the dustbins.

The same habits give you the quickest tips to clean house before guests arrive: clear the entrance, wipe the guest bathroom, and keep the coffee table empty, which takes about 15 minutes when clutter has nowhere to build up. Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to declutter a 2 BHK before Diwali?

Working one zone at a time for 30 to 60 minutes, most families clear a 2 BHK in six to eight sessions spread over two weekends and a few weekday evenings. The loft and the wardrobes take the longest, so give each its own session. Start in mid October so the decluttering is done before the deep cleaning and pest control in early November.

Where can I give away old clothes and household items in India?

Wearable clothes and working utensils can go to a local clothes donation drive, a place of worship that collects donations, or a neighbourhood NGO collection point. Ask your building's housekeeping or security staff too, as many are glad to take useful items. Newspapers, cartons and scrap metal go to the kabadiwala, who pays by weight.

Should I declutter before or after Diwali deep cleaning?

Declutter first, always. A cleaner can only scrub the shelves, floors and lofts they can reach, and anything you were going to give away would otherwise be dusted and moved for nothing. Aim to finish about two weeks before Diwali.

What should I do with old idols, puja items and religious calendars?

Follow your own family custom here, since practices differ between families and regions. Many families take worn idols, old calendars and used puja items to a temple that accepts them, or to a collection point set up for respectful disposal near the festival.

Is it safe to use naphthalene balls in wardrobes after decluttering?

Naphthalene and camphor can be harmful to babies, young children and pets, so keep them in sealed sachets, out of reach, and never where food is stored. Follow the label on the pack. Dry neem leaves or cedar blocks are gentler options in a wardrobe, and clean, fully dry clothes are the best defence against moths.
