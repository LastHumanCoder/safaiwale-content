How to Prevent Mosquitoes at Home: 12 Practical Ways to Try

If you want to know how to prevent mosquitoes at home, start with water: mosquitoes lay their eggs in still water, and a bucket, cooler or plant saucer left alone for a week can produce a fresh batch. Most of how to prevent mosquitoes at home comes down to a weekly habit of emptying, scrubbing and covering, backed by mesh, nets and the right repellent. This guide sets out how to prevent mosquitoes at home in 12 practical ways, beginning with a standing-water checklist, then barriers, repellents and a short note on essential oils. Knowing how to prevent mosquitoes at home also means knowing when a fever after bites needs a doctor, which we cover near the end.

Why does standing water matter more than sprays?

A spray kills the mosquitoes flying in the room tonight. Standing water produces next week's. In warm weather, a mosquito can go from egg to adult in about a week, so emptying every container once a week breaks the cycle before new adults appear.

The mosquito that spreads dengue, Aedes, prefers clean water stored in and around homes: buckets, coolers, tanks and pots. The World Health Organization notes that this mosquito breeds mainly in man-made containers. It also glues its eggs to the inside wall of a container just above the waterline, which is why tipping water out is not enough. You have to scrub the sides.

The standing-water checklist: ways 1 to 6

Walk through your home with this list every Sunday. It does more than any repellent you can buy.

1. Empty and scrub stored-water containers

Buckets, drums, matkas and any water kept for cleaning or plants need emptying, a scrub with a stiff brush on the inner walls, and a tight lid. If you must store water for several days, cover it with a fitted lid or a cloth tied tight, not one balanced on top.

2. Drain and dry the desert cooler

Coolers are one of the most common breeding spots in North Indian homes. Drain the tank fully once a week, scrub it, let it dry for a few hours and refill. When the cooler is off for the season, empty and dry it completely and cover it.

3. Fix plant saucers, vases and money plant bottles

Change the water in vases and money plant bottles every week and rinse the inside. Fill plant saucers with sand so excess water soaks in instead of pooling, or empty them a few hours after watering.

4. Cover overhead tanks and sumps

Check that tank lids close fully and that the overflow and vent pipes have mesh over the openings. A cracked lid on a rooftop tank can breed mosquitoes for a whole building. If the tank has not been cleaned in a while, our water tank cleaning service handles the emptying and scrubbing for large or underground tanks.

5. Clean the fridge drip tray and AC drain

The tray at the back or base of many fridges collects defrost water and gets forgotten for months. Pull it out and wash it weekly. Check that the AC drain pipe empties into a drain or a pot you tip out, not onto a ledge where water pools.

6. Clear the roof, balcony and outdoor clutter

Old tyres, broken pots, coconut shells, tins and folds in plastic sheets all hold rainwater. Throw out what you do not need, turn the rest upside down, and clear leaves from balcony drains and gutters so water runs off.

Which barriers keep mosquitoes out: ways 7 and 8?

7. Fit mesh on windows and doors

Fine insect mesh on windows, balcony doors and ventilators stops most mosquitoes from entering. Check it each season for tears and gaps at the frame, and close doors without mesh around dusk, when many mosquitoes become active.

8. Sleep under a mosquito net

A bed net is still one of the safest ways to protect babies, young children and older family members at night, because it needs no chemical near the face. Tuck the edges under the mattress and check for holes before each season.

What is the best mosquito repellent for home use: ways 9 to 12?

The best mosquito repellent for home depends on the room, who sleeps in it and whether you can keep a window open. Whatever you choose, read the label for age limits and ventilation.

9. Liquid vaporisers and mats

Plug-in vaporisers and mats suit bedrooms and living rooms. Switch them on a while before you sleep, keep a window or door slightly open, and place them away from a baby's cot rather than beside the face. Switch off when the room is empty.

10. Skin repellents for time outdoors

Creams, lotions and roll-ons help in the evening on a balcony, a terrace or a park walk. Apply them on exposed skin only, not under clothing or on cuts, and do not apply on a young child's hands, which go into the mouth. Check the minimum age on the pack before using any repellent on a child.

11. Use coils only in open spaces

Coils release smoke as they burn. Use them on an open balcony or terrace, never in a closed bedroom, and keep them away from anyone with asthma or a cough, and from curtains.

12. Time your habits to the bite

The dengue mosquito bites mostly in the daytime, with peaks in the early morning and late afternoon, while malaria mosquitoes bite mainly at night. Long sleeves and full-length trousers at those hours help, and a ceiling or pedestal fan over a seating area makes it harder for mosquitoes to land, as they are weak fliers.

Do essential oils and home remedies for mosquitoes work?

Some essential oils to repel insects, such as citronella, lemongrass, eucalyptus and neem, do keep mosquitoes away for a short time. The effect wears off faster than a registered repellent, so you need to reapply often, and they cannot replace the water checklist. Other home remedies for mosquitoes, like a tulsi plant near the window, give a mild effect at best.

  • Always dilute an oil in a carrier such as coconut oil before it touches skin, and patch test on the inner arm first.
  • Do not use essential oils on babies or toddlers, and do not run a diffuser in a closed room where an infant sleeps.
  • Many oils can be harmful to pets, especially cats, so keep bottles and diffusers out of their space.
  • Camphor is often burned to drive mosquitoes out. It is toxic if swallowed, so keep camphor tablets well out of reach of children, and ventilate the room after burning it.

When do mosquito bites need a doctor?

Most bites only itch. A fever in the days or weeks after mosquito bites is different: see a doctor rather than waiting it out, because dengue, malaria and other mosquito-borne illnesses can start with fever, body ache, headache or chills, and only a test can tell them apart. Do not self-medicate; let the doctor decide on tests and medicine. Go to a hospital quickly if someone with fever has severe stomach pain, keeps vomiting, bleeds from the gums or nose, has trouble breathing or seems unusually drowsy. The National Center for Vector Borne Diseases Control publishes India's public guidance on these diseases. Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I check my home for standing water?

Once a week is the right rhythm, because mosquito eggs can become adults in about a week in warm weather. Pick a fixed day, such as Sunday morning, and go through the cooler, buckets, plant saucers, fridge tray and balcony. After heavy rain, do an extra round the next day.

Are mosquito vaporisers safe to use in a baby's room?

Follow the label, which states any age limits and how much ventilation the product needs. Keep the device away from the cot, leave a window or door slightly open, and switch it off when it is not needed. For babies, a well-fitted mosquito net over the cot is the safer first choice because it uses no chemical at all.

Why are there more mosquitoes after the monsoon?

Rain leaves water in pots, tyres, terraces, construction sites and blocked drains, and the warm, humid weather that follows speeds up breeding. Many homes also keep coolers and storage drums filled through this period. Weekly emptying through September, October and November makes a clear difference in your own home.

Does fogging my building stop mosquitoes for good?

No. Fogging kills adult mosquitoes it reaches at the time of spraying, but it does not touch eggs and larvae sitting in water, so new adults appear within days if breeding spots remain. It works best as a short-term measure alongside source reduction in the building and a regular check of tanks, drains and common areas.

Can I use neem oil on my skin to keep mosquitoes away?

Neem oil can give short protection if you dilute a few drops in coconut oil and patch test it first. It wears off faster than a registered repellent, so reapply it often, and do not use it on babies. Stop using it if your skin turns red, itchy or irritated.
